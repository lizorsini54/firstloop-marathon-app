# Glute Gladiator: Revamped
## 12-Week Strength + Physique Program (4 Days/Week)

Built for: 15 years lifting experience, glute/lower-body emphasis with balanced full physique, running as an equal training priority, shin splint history (zero jump/plyo work — running covers all impact needs).

---

## How This Differs From the Original

- **Standard barbell/dumbbell/cable movements only.** No banded frog pump circuits, no burpee finishers, no gimmicks. Every exercise earns its slot.
- **Real progression.** Three 4-week blocks moving from hypertrophy to strength, with double progression inside each block and planned deloads. The original's "all sets heavier than last week" is not a progression model.
- **Run-compatible.** No plyometrics, no metcon abs, no stair master finishers. Your runs are your conditioning. Lifting fatigue is managed so quality runs stay quality.
- **Shin-aware.** Direct calf and tibialis work twice a week as prehab, kept light and high-rep.

## Weekly Layout

| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| Monday | Lower A: Glute + Hinge Strength |
| Tuesday | Upper A: Push Emphasis |
| Wednesday | Run (quality day OK — legs are 24h out from Lower A) |
| Thursday | Lower B: Squat + Glute Volume |
| Friday | Run (easy) or rest |
| Saturday | Upper B: Pull Emphasis |
| Sunday | Long run |

Adjust freely, but protect two rules: **48+ hours between Lower A and Lower B**, and **no Lower B the day before a long run or workout run.** Upper days can sit anywhere.

---

## Block Structure

| Block | Weeks | Main Lift Reps | Accessory Reps | Intent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1: Build | 1–3 | 8–10 | 10–15 | Hypertrophy, movement quality, base volume |
| Deload | 4 | 8 (light) | 12 (light) | 60–70% of week 3 loads, drop 1 set everywhere |
| 2: Strengthen | 5–7 | 6–8 | 8–12 | Heavier loading, same movements |
| Deload | 8 | 6 (light) | 10 (light) | Same deload rules |
| 3: Peak | 9–11 | 4–6 | 8–10 | Heaviest work of the cycle |
| Test/Deload | 12 | Work to a heavy top set on hip thrust, squat, RDL, bench, row | — | Set new rep maxes, then rest |

**Progression rule (double progression):** Pick a weight at the bottom of the rep range. Add reps each week until you hit the top of the range on all sets, then add weight (5 lb upper, 10 lb lower) and start over. Main lifts should end around RPE 8 (2 reps in reserve); accessories RPE 8–9.

## Starting Weight Anchors (From Your Current Logs)

- Hip Thrust: start Block 1 around 165–175 x 10 (you're at 185 x 8)
- RDL: start around 105–115 x 10 (95 x 10 is leaving reps in the tank)
- Sumo Deadlift: 125 x 10 was comfortable for you at volume, so 6–8 rep work can start around 140–155
- Squat and bench: start at a true RPE 7 for the block's rep target and let double progression do the work

## The Pull-Up Project: 0–3 → 10+ This Year, 20 Next Year

Pull-up progress is driven by **frequency at submaximal effort**, not by going to failure once a week. The engine of this plan is short, easy sets done often; Upper B is where the harder work lives.

### Honest Timeline

| Milestone | Target |
|---|---|
| 5 strict reps | ~8–10 weeks |
| 8–10 strict reps | End of 2026 |
| 15 strict reps | Mid 2027 |
| 20 strict reps | Late 2027 |

Progress from 0–3 to 10 moves fast. 10 to 20 is a long grind where every rep is earned. Body composition matters too: pull-ups are strength-to-weight, so marathon training leanness works in your favor.

### Daily Practice (Grease the Groove)

On 4–5 days per week, do 3–5 mini-sets spread through the day (or spread across a single hour if that's more practical):

- **Set size: about half your current max, minimum 1.** At a max of 3, that means singles and doubles.
- Never to failure. Every rep crisp: dead hang start, chest toward bar, controlled down.
- Rest days from this: day before and day of your long run, plus one more.
- If you don't have bar access during the day, do all sets before/after your regular sessions.

### Phase Progression (Upper B Heavy Work)

| Phase | Trigger | Upper B Protocol |
|---|---|---|
| 1: Foundation | Max is 0–5 | 5 x 3–5 negatives (5-second lowering) + 3 x 6–8 band-assisted pull-ups with the lightest band that allows clean reps |
| 2: Volume | Max hits 5 | Ladder sets: 1-2-3, repeat for 3–4 rounds, 60–90 sec between rungs. Retest max every 2 weeks. |
| 3: Density | Max hits 8 | Total-rep target: 25 reps in as few sets as possible, add 2 reps to the target each week |
| 4: Ceiling | Max hits 10–12 | Add weighted pull-ups (3 x 3–5) before density work. Weighted strength is what pulls a 12 max toward 20. |

### Rules

1. Retest your true max every 2 weeks, fresh, at the start of Upper B. Log it.
2. When your max changes, your grease-the-groove set size changes (half of new max).
3. Elbow or shoulder ache = drop grease-the-groove to 2 days for a week. Tendons adapt slower than muscles, and this is the most common way this goal gets derailed.
4. Chin-ups (palms facing you) count during Phases 1–2 if they let you do cleaner reps. Transition to overhand by Phase 3.

## Running Interference Rules

1. Never max-effort lower body within 24 hours before a quality run (tempo, intervals, long run).
2. If a run felt terrible and legs are trashed, cut lower day accessories, keep the main lift.
3. During peak marathon mileage weeks (later in your 39-week plan), drop to 3 lifting days: Lower A, Upper A, Upper B. Lower B is the first thing to cut.
4. Deload weeks here should align with your run plan's down weeks when possible.
